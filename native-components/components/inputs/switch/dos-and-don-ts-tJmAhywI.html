Switch

Switches are controls that allow users to make a binary on/off choice. Their input is immediate and requires no additional action.

  • Overview
  • Dos & Don'ts

Properties

 

Label

A label is required for all switches. The label should clearly indicate what is being activated.

 

Secondary text

Switches can include secondary text (subcopy) beneath the main label to provide additional context. This is helpful when the primary label alone may not fully explain the option.

 

Divider

Dividers are required when placing multiple switches in a list format.

 

 

 

Usage

 

Rewards

Use a switch when turning an option on or off gives an immediate result, like applying Trip Cash to the booking total.

 

Notification preferences

Use switches for persistent settings that control ongoing behaviors, such as receiving notifications.

 

 

 

Behavior

 

Label overflow

Labels should be kept short, but will wrap if they are wider than the container. The switch remains aligned to the top of the text box.

 

Avoid using the words “on” or “off” within the label. Switches imply an on/off state so including it in the label is redundant. 

 

Text wrapping

By default, content starts to truncate after a certain number of lines as follows:

Immediate

Toggling a switch shows immediate results.

 

 

 

Best practices

 

Label

Do

Use clear and concise labels.

Don't

Do not use action-related verbs with “on” or “off” when adding labels to controls.

 

Placement

Do

Always place switches as the trailing action.

Don't

Do not use switches as leading actions.

 

Radio button vs Switch

Do

Switches are used for activation. If you are selecting between two available options, use a radio button instead.

Don't

Do not use switches to choose between opposing or discrete options.

 

Checkbox vs Switch

Do

Use a checkbox for an option saved after a final button is tapped.

Don't

Do not use a switch when there is a final button to submit the selection. Use a switch from immediate effect.
